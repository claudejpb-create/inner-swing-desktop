# Inner Swing — Assembly Instructions (prototype)

These instructions describe the prototype **as built**: a wooden box whose lid carries a small
playground-style swing. Copper A-frame legs and hardwood top blocks hold a brass axle, and the axle is
glued straight onto a hidden stepper motor. Parts and sizes are in [BOM.md](BOM.md).

- All dimensions are in **millimetres**.
- **Build to the numbers, but trust your parts.** Every box, tube and motor differs slightly. Where a size depends on your parts, the step says what to measure.
- **Try every new drill setting on scrap first.**
- **Templates:** printable 1:1 templates are in `inner_swing_templates_1to1.pdf`:
  - lid drilling,
  - A-frame check,
  - cut sheet,
  - top blocks.
- Wear safety glasses at every machine, and clamp small pieces instead of holding them.

---

## 0. The design at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Base | Wooden box 199 × 150 × 79, right way up. Its **lid (11 thick) is the swing platform**, stiffened by a 178 × 129 × 12 plywood plate glued underneath. The electronics hang under the plate; lift the lid to reach them. |
| Frame | Four ⅜″ (9.5 mm) copper legs leaning **12°**, forming an A-frame at each **narrow** end of the box. Frame centres are 159 apart (20 in from each end). |
| Top blocks | Two hardwood blocks, 48 wide × 44 tall × 26 deep, round top (R18), sides flaring 12° into the legs. The **back block** hides the motor in a pocket; the **front block** holds the free end of the axle. The blocks' inner faces are **133 apart**. |
| Axle | **6 mm brass tube glued directly onto the 5 mm motor shaft.** No coupler and no bushing. The free end turns in a waxed 6.5 mm blind hole in the front block. |
| Seat | 52 × 22 × 5 hardwood, hung on two 2.5 mm brass rods 42 apart. The rods pass through the axle. The seat top hangs 150 below the axle centre. |
| Wiring | The motor wires run down inside the back-left leg (the **wire leg**) to the electronics under the lid. |
| Overall | Axle about 180 above the lid; overall height about 269. |

**How it moves:** the axle is the top bar, and it turns. The seat rods are glued through the axle, so when
the motor turns a few degrees, the axle and seat turn with it.

---

## 1. Tools

- Drill press. It doesn't need to tilt: see the 12° ramp jig in step 3.
- Bits:
  - 10 mm (or ⅜″) brad-point;
  - 30 mm Forstner, plus a **spare 30 mm Forstner with its centre spur ground off** (step 5);
  - 6.5 mm, 6 mm and 2.6–2.7 mm twist bits.
- Band saw or mitre saw; disc, belt or spindle sander; a small chisel.
- Mini tube cutter for the ⅜″ copper; junior hacksaw for the brass; small files; centre punch.
- A small V-block (or a scrap with a V-groove) to hold the axle for drilling.
- Clamps, masking tape, a square, a ruler, and a phone angle app (for example Clinometer or Bubble Level).
- Tin snips (motor tabs), and a sewing needle or pin (to free the motor wires from their plug).

---

## 2. Before the shop

1. **Measure your box:** outside size with the lid on, the lid thickness, the box opening, and the inside depth. The prototype box is 199 × 150 × 79, with an 11 mm lid, a 182 × 133 opening and 60 deep inside.
2. **Check the motor.** Hold it with the shaft at the top. The blue plastic wire cap should be at the bottom, opposite the shaft.
3. **Practise freeing a wire from the motor plug.** The wires will go down a copper leg without the plug.
   1. Photograph the plug first, showing the wire colours in order (blue, pink, yellow, orange, red).
   2. Each wire's metal contact has a small springy tab showing through a slot in the plug. Press it with a needle while gently pulling the wire; the wire comes out with its contact.
   3. Push it back into the same hole until it clicks.
4. **Check the wires fit the leg:** slip all five freed wires through a short piece of ⅜″ tube. If they don't fit, use ½″ tube for the wire leg only, with 13 mm holes for that leg.
5. **Measure the motor shaft:** 5 mm diameter. Check that your 6 mm × 0.5 mm-wall brass tube (5.0 inside) slides snugly onto it.

---

## 3. The lid

1. **Make the lid sit flat.** Rub pencil on the edges of the lid's raised panel, set it on, press, and lift it off. Sand wherever the pencil smudged, and repeat until the lid drops fully all round.
2. **Glue the plywood plate**, 178 × 129 × 12, centred under the lid's raised panel. Clamp it overnight, then check the lid still drops onto the box.
3. **Mark the four leg holes** on masking tape on the lid's top (template page 1):
   - **20 in from each end** (159 apart, end to end);
   - **27.5 in from the front and back edges** (95 apart, front to back);
   - label the **back hole at the left end** (seen from the long front side) **WIRE**.
4. **Make a 12° ramp jig**, if your drill press table doesn't tilt.
   - Prototype: a board 185 × 195, with two wedges 150 long × 33 high under it (≈ 12.4°), and a stop strip along the low edge.
   - Check the angle with a phone app, or measure a rise of 21 mm per 100 mm.
5. **Placing the lid on the ramp:** the long edge nearest the hole you're drilling goes on the low side, against the stop. Each hole then slants toward the middle of the lid, front to back, the way its leg leans.
6. **Drill a test hole in scrap** with the 10 mm brad-point bit. A tube should slide in with a little effort and lean 12°.
7. **Drill the holes** through the lid and the plate. Use a depth stop about 5 mm into a backer board.
   - **WIRE hole:** right through.
   - **The other three:** either right through, or **blind** (stopped part way), so the leg rests on the bottom of the hole. The prototype uses blind holes.
   - **Record the depth of each blind hole**; you need it for the leg lengths.
8. **Check from below:** with the lid on the box, the WIRE hole comes out under the plate, clear of the box walls.

---

## 4. Copper legs

1. **Leg lengths:**
   - **Wire leg:** 181 (through the lid and plate, with 8 inside the top block).
   - **Each other leg:** **157 + the depth of its own blind hole**. Make each one to its hole.
2. **Cut** with the tube cutter: tighten a little, turn the cutter around the tube, and repeat until the tube parts. Cut one leg, test it, then cut the rest.
3. **Deburr** the inside of each end, especially the wire leg, so sharp edges don't cut the wire insulation.
4. **Polish** with #0000 steel wool along the length and wipe with alcohol. Spray 2–3 light coats of clear lacquer, leaving about 2 cm bare and lightly roughened at each end so the epoxy grips.

---

## 5. Top blocks, cover and seat

Cut rectangles first and drill all the holes **before** shaping: rectangles are easier to clamp. Template page 4
gives the outline at 1:1. A strip method for making several blocks at once is described at the end.

1. **Cut:**
   - front block 48 × 44 × 26;
   - back block 48 × 44 × 23;
   - cover 48 × 44 × 3;
   - seat 52 × 22 × 5.

   Mark each block FRONT or BACK, its top, and its **inner face** (the face toward the seat).
2. **Axle line:** on each inner face, mark a point centred side to side and **10 down from the top**.
   - **Front block:** drill **6.5 mm, 20 deep** (not through). The axle's free end turns in this hole.
   - **Back block:** drill **10 mm right through**, for the motor shaft and its raised boss.
3. **Leg sockets** (10 mm, at 12° on the ramp):
   - on each block's bottom, two centres **16.5 either side of the middle** (33 apart) and **13 from the inner face**;
   - **8 deep**, slanting toward the middle of the block as they go up.

   Looking at the back block's back face, the wire leg is on your **left**.
4. **Motor pocket** (back block):
   - on the back face, centred side to side and **18 down from the top** (the centre of the R18 top);
   - **Ø30 × 19 deep**, leaving a **4 mm** wall. The 10 mm shaft hole comes out near the top of the pocket floor.
   - **The Forstner's centre spur limits the depth:** it pierces the wall before the pocket is deep enough. Make the first pass with a normal 30 mm Forstner, then finish with a **spare 30 mm Forstner whose spur is ground flush** with its cutting edges.
     - **Grinding:** short touches on a grinder, dipping the bit in water often.
     - **Holding the block:** the existing pocket guides the bit. Clamp a plywood backer to the drill press table. With the power off, lower the bit into the pocket to centre the block. Hot-glue three scrap stops to the backer around the block, and clamp a thin hold-down stick across the solid strip below the pocket.
     - **Depth:** in two steps, test-fitting the motor between them. Use slow speed and lift often.
   - **The motor should sit flush with the back face, or up to 0.5 below it.**
   - **If the wall would end up thinner than 2 mm:**
     - make the cover thicker instead (about 7 mm, with a Ø30 recess for the part of the motor that sticks out); or
     - drill a Ø30 hole straight through and use the 3D-printed motor cradle (`inner_swing_motor_cradle.stl`).
5. **Prepare the motor:** cut off both mounting tabs close to the body and file the edges smooth.
6. **Wire-cap notch:** fit the motor (shaft through the 10 mm hole, blue cap pointing down), mark around the cap, then chisel a notch for it, about 16 wide × 4 deep and the full pocket depth.
7. **Shape the tops**, on each block's big face:
   1. Draw a circle of **R18**, centred side to side and 18 down from the top.
   2. Mark the bottom corners **24 either side** of the centre line (48 apart).
   3. From each bottom corner, draw a line that just touches the circle. These lines lean 12°.
   4. Tape the cover to the back block's back face and shape them together, so they match.
   5. Band-saw just outside the lines, sand to the lines, and soften every edge (about 2 mm round).
8. **Wire channel:** on the back block's back face, chisel a channel about 6 wide × 9 deep from the cap notch to the top of the wire-leg socket. The cover hides it.
9. **Cover magnets:** drill two shallow holes in the cover's inside face, over the motor's steel case, and epoxy in two small neodymium magnets. The cover then sticks to the motor.
10. **Seat:** drill two **2.6–2.7 mm** holes right through, **42 apart** (5 in from each end), centred, with a backer underneath.
11. **Sand** everything to 220 grit. **Oil** the wood (hardwax or Danish oil), keeping oil out of the glue holes.

---

## 6. Axle and hangers

The axle is the **6 mm brass tube itself**, glued straight onto the motor shaft.

1. **Length:** dry-fit the motor in its pocket and both blocks on the 133 spacer (step 7). Measure from the **bottom of the front block's 6.5 mm hole** to the **raised ring at the base of the motor shaft**, and subtract **1 mm**. That's about **154** in the prototype.
2. **Cut** the tube with a junior hacksaw and file the ends square and smooth.
3. **Filler rod:** push a **5 mm rod or dowel, 50 long**, inside the tube to sit under the hanger holes. It stops the thin tube from crushing when drilled and gives the glue something to grip.
4. **Hanger holes:** mark **65.5 and 107.5 from the front (free) end** (42 apart), on one straight line along the tube.
   - Centre-punch each mark, then clamp the tube in a V-block.
   - Drill **2.6–2.7 mm** right through at each mark. Slide the tube along the V-block between holes without turning it, so the holes stay parallel.
5. **Hangers:** cut two **2.5 mm** brass rods to **175**.
   - Roughen the last 1 cm of each, then epoxy one end into each seat hole, with about 1 mm poking out underneath.
   - Prop the rods straight and parallel against a square while the glue sets.
   - File the ends under the seat into small rounded "dots."
6. **Wax** the free end of the axle and the front block's 6.5 mm hole, so it turns freely.

---

## 7. Assembly

**Do a complete dry run first, with no glue.** Mix 30-minute epoxy only once every step works.

1. **Legs into the lid.** Set the lid on two scraps. Push each leg into its own hole: the three into their blind holes, and the wire leg through its hole.
2. **Spacer:** cut a scrap exactly **133** long. It holds the two blocks' inner faces at the right distance.
3. **Motor and axle into the back block:**
   - **Free the five motor wires** from their plug (photo first), and feed them, contact first, from the pocket through the cap notch, along the wire channel and down the wire leg into the box.
   - **Seat the motor** in its pocket, blue cap in the notch.
   - **Glue the axle onto the shaft:** a thin coat of epoxy inside the tube's motor end, then push it fully onto the motor shaft, up to the shaft's raised ring.
4. **Blocks onto the legs:** with the spacer between the blocks and the axle's free end in the front block's hole, lower both blocks onto the four leg tops. Each leg enters its socket at the same 12° slant, and the legs flex about 2 mm to let the blocks drop on.
   - **When gluing:** a little epoxy in each socket and each lid hole. Keep epoxy out of the inside of the wire leg.
   - **Before it sets:** check from the front and the side that the blocks are upright, level and in line, with a straightedge across their tops. Turn the axle by hand: it should move smoothly. Leave it overnight.
5. **Seat on:** from below, push the two rod tops up through the axle holes until the seat top is **150 below the axle centre** and level.
   - Put a small drop of CA glue where each rod passes through the axle.
   - When set, cut the rod tops about 1 mm above the axle and file them into small rounded rivets.
6. **Rebuild the motor plug** under the lid: click each contact back into its original hole, in the order from your photo.
7. **Cover on:** the magnets hold it.

---

## 8. Electronics

The wiring table is in [BOM.md](BOM.md#wiring-prototype).

1. **Mount the boards under the plywood plate** on short standoffs or Velcro. That gives about 43 of height.
   - **ULN2003 driver:** near the wire-leg hole.
   - **UNO:** wherever its USB cable can reach the box's USB port (or the power bank).
2. **Connect:**
   - ULN2003 IN1–IN4 to UNO pins 8, 9, 10, 11, and ULN2003 + / − to UNO 5V / GND;
   - the motor plug into the driver's white socket.
3. **HM-10 Bluetooth** (when fitted):
   - VCC / GND to the UNO;
   - **TXD → pin 2**;
   - **pin 3 → 1 kΩ → RXD → 2 kΩ → GND**. The divider protects the 3.3 V input.
   - Keep the HM-10 away from the steel washers.
4. **USB / power:**
   - **Laptop testing:** a USB cable through a round hole in the box wall.
   - **Standalone use:** a round USB-B panel connector in the wall serves as the charging port for an "Always On" power bank under the lid (BOM section 3).
5. **Ballast:** glue 3–4 large steel washers to the box floor, clear of the boards and the HM-10, so the swing can't tip.

## 9. Firmware and first swing

1. **Install the Arduino IDE.** Open `InnerSwing/InnerSwing.ino`, select **Arduino Uno** and its COM port, and click **Upload**. Close the Arduino IDE afterwards: it holds the USB port.
2. **Let the seat hang straight down**, then plug in USB. The position at power-up becomes the centre.
3. **Resting sway:** after a second the swing begins a gentle sway at the stored pace (3.5 breaths per minute by default).
4. **Serial Monitor test** (9600 baud, "New Line"):

   | Type | Result |
   |---|---|
   | `?` | Replies with the version and stored pace |
   | `R6` | Sets the pace to 6/min (stored; valid range 3–7) |
   | `N1` / `N-1` | Nudges the centre by 1° |

5. **Direction:** if the swing moves opposite to the pendulum on the page, either turn the swing around, or set `REVERSE = true` in the sketch and upload again.

---

## 10. Using it with the web page

1. Open `rsa_pendulum.html` in **Chrome or Edge**: from a file on a computer, or from an **https** address on an Android phone (phones only allow Bluetooth on https pages).
2. **Connect strap.** It needs a strap that sends RR intervals, for example a Polar H10.
3. **Connect the swing:**
   - **Connect swing** (HM-10 Bluetooth), or
   - **Swing by USB** (laptop, before Bluetooth is fitted).
4. **Begin session** and breathe with the swing. The swing grows when your heart rate rises and falls **in step with the swing's breathing**, and returns to a small arc when you breathe normally.

## Appendix: making blocks in batches (strip method)

The block outline is the same through its whole depth. So for several swings:
1. Mill a hardwood strip 48 × 44 in section, 300–400 long, with the grain along the strip.
2. Cut the 12° flares on the table saw.
3. Round the top along the full length.
4. Sand the whole strip.
5. Crosscut the blocks: 26 for front blocks; 23 plus 3 for back blocks and their covers.
6. Drill with fixed stops on the 12° ramp, so every block sits in the same place.

One 350 mm strip makes about 6 swings.

## Appendix: notes

- **Keep the swing light.** The seat and rods together should weigh about 20 g or less, to stay well within the motor's strength.
- **Replacing the motor:** the axle is glued to the shaft, so it comes out with the motor. Free the wires at the plug, pull them up out of the leg, and fit a new motor with a new axle.
- **Copper darkens where the lacquer is thin.** To restore it: remove the lacquer with acetone, polish, and re-lacquer.
- **Gearbox play:** the motor's small gearbox has a little play. If the seat lurches slightly as it passes the bottom, a touch of friction on the axle's free end (a felt washer, or less wax) steadies it.
